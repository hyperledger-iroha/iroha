# Private backing disposal

The exclusive `MemoryImage` owner zeroizes every initialized byte before its local boxed allocation or funded fixed buffer deallocates. Published images initialize their full capacity; late construction failure also destroys the already initialized image through this owner. Inline register values and tags are zeroized in `Registers::drop`.

Runtime templates pair a standard data Arc with a separate reservation owner. Every handle declares and drops the data Arc first. Its final payload is destroyed in place, so inline registers are wiped inside the original shared allocation. The paired reservation owner holds both exact Arc layouts and remaining original execution credit until the data Arc, its private payloads and its own backing have been released. Every clone retains both references; data/Weak/raw Arc handles are not exposed. The generic reservation `StrongOwner` remains unchanged.

Both metadata Arc layouts are included in active admission and aggregate retention accounting. Physical Arc construction still uses the existing infallible `Arc::new` allocation path after reservation; graceful allocator failure of those control allocations remains an open gate.

The fixed local diagnostic recorders volatile-erase initialized retained register values/tags, memory-access values and their optional complete initial image before backing deallocation. A memory recorder attached to a VM remains readable by its other handles until its final shared owner drops; erasure never changes the source VM or a template baseline. Step completion clears its explicit append temporary and pending owner, and terminal replacement clears the previous retained register snapshot first. Row backing remains funded by its original reservation until physical deallocation. The small shared recorder control allocation is still outside this plan.

Production ZK-lane capture now creates one move-only `DiagnosticTraceSnapshot` through the State-owned execution pool. Its checked plan includes expanded register states, constraints, both event descriptor tables, every nested authentication sibling and step records. All fixed backing is admitted and allocated before any private data is copied. Consumers borrow event views and register states; there are no allocating `register_log`/`register_trace` exports or a cloning `ZkTask`. The borrowed checker uses one canonical Merkle verifier and requires exactly eight siblings for the 256-register tree.

Every original snapshot allocation retains its charge through physical destruction. The final snapshot owner volatile-erases initialized scalar values, privacy tags and path material before backing deallocation. Capture never erases the source VM. Optional diagnostic admission refusal skips background submission without affecting guest validity, gas or effects. Retry expiry and queue clearing detach owners under the queue mutex and perform digest cleanup and final destruction after unlocking. Each cleanup pass is bounded to its initial cohort; concurrently enqueued owners remain retained.

This boundary does not erase caller-created `Copy` snapshots, live source-log allocations and growth, host buffers, compiler-generated temporary copies or other Merkle-derived digest storage. The `Registers` hash tree and `ByteMerkleTree` leaves/nodes are not zeroized by this cut. Their final-owner cleanup remains separate, and production incomplete-proof and raw-private-input admission stay closed.
