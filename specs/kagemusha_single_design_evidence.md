# KAGEMUSHA single design — evidence appendix

Companion to [`kagemusha_single_design_proposal.md`](kagemusha_single_design_proposal.md).
Compiled 2026-10-02.

Section 0 is the claim map for revision 5 of the proposal and is the place to
start. Sections 1 to 6 are the research behind revisions 2 to 4, kept as
history under the section numbers of the revision each was written for.
Section 7 lists sources the revision-5 drafts added.

## How to read this

- Every row in sections 1 to 4 was produced by an automated research pass and
  re-checked by a second automated pass. Sections 5 and 6 had one research
  pass and one adversarial pass; see the last item of this list. Nothing was built or run, and no human expert has
  reviewed it. Treat each row as a pointer to check, not as a verified result.
- URLs were fetched on 2026-10-02 unless a row says otherwise. Repository
  references are relative to the repository root on branch `optimizations` and
  may drift.
- **Kind** classifies each claim:
  - `documented`: a primary vendor or standards source says it.
  - `repo-measured`: a measurement recorded in this repository.
  - `repo-code`: read from source code; not executed.
  - `third-party`: a non-primary report.
  - `extrapolation`: computed or scaled from other figures; the assumption is
    stated.
  - `inference`: reasoning, not sourced.
- The tables in sections 2 and 3 were compiled against **revision 2** of the
  proposal: section numbers, stage names and quoted wording are revision 2's
  (its stage Q0 is revision 3's Q0a, which is narrower). Rows may quote
  sentences revision 3 no longer contains.
- Section 1 lists the revision-2 statements the sources contradicted, could
  not support, or supported only with a qualifier. Some entries record that no
  change was needed. It does not record item by item how revision 3 changed
  each one.
- Where section 2 and section 3 disagree (rows P11, P16, P20 and I19), section
  3 is the later reading and revision 3 follows it.
- Row I9 and the Android clock row in section 2 record revision 2's "a reboot
  loses the measured interval". Revision 3 §5.4 supersedes both; its sources
  are in section 4.
- Section 4 holds rows for statements first made in revision 3. Its URLs were
  cited by the automated check of revision 2 and were not fetched again.
- Sections 5 and 6 hold rows for revision 4 of the proposal: the comparison
  with the June 2025 TC3 document, and the study of counters and commitments
  behind its §4.1. Each was produced by one automated research pass and one
  automated adversarial pass on 2026-10-02.
- A few source references point to "session research notes". Those were working
  notes, not repository files; the primary source is given beside them wherever
  one exists.


## 0. Current claim map (revision 5)

This section maps the factual claims the proposal makes to their sources. It
is organised by proposal section. Read it in place of sections 1 to 6. Those
sections were written for earlier revisions and stay below as history.

What it covers. Facts about platforms, the repository, published work and the
June 2025 document. It does not list design rules. A rule is a choice, not a
fact. Where a rule rests on a fact, the fact has a row.

How it was made. One automated pass compiled it on 2026-10-02 from the older
sections and opened part of the sources again. Nothing was built, run or
tested on a device. No human expert has checked it. A row is a pointer to
check, not a verified result.

Columns.

- **Id**: `C-<proposal section>-<n>`.
- **Claim**: one sentence, as the proposal should state it. In a contradicted
  row the Claim is the sentence as the proposal last stated it, in quotation
  marks, and the Status gives the corrected sentence.
- **Source**: a URL, a repository path and line, or "none". Every URL was
  fetched on 2026-10-02. Apple reference pages were read through their JSON
  form; the human URL is listed. Repository lines are those of commit
  `b2a3cd05bc` on branch `optimizations` unless a row names the working tree.
  "app. N" names the older section of this appendix that holds the row, with
  the row's label where it has one.
- **Class**: as defined under "How to read this". "Source reading" means the
  behaviour was inferred from code and not observed.
- **Passes**: how many automated passes opened the source.
  - `2`: a research pass and a second check.
  - `1`: one research pass. A later adversarial pass argued against the claim
    without opening the source again.
  - `m`: first sourced by the pass that compiled this map.
  - A trailing `+m` means the pass that compiled this map opened the source
    again.
  - Rows marked `1` or `m` rest on one automated pass. A dash means no source
    was opened.
- **Status**: `supported`; `supported, limit:` with the limit; `unsupported`;
  or `contradicted`, followed by the corrected sentence.

- §2.2 'App Attest and KeyMint platform classes are rejected at every boundary' is overbroad. ### prior-art

- §2.1 item 3, 'Per-hop relation plus hardware uniqueness: prevention' — supported only with a qualifier. The sources that document hardware-anchored prevention also say it fails once the hardware itself is broken: Allen et al. §8.3 and PayOff §III-B (one compromised device spends without limit), Bank of England 2025 (if secure elements were breached, double spending and counterfeiting might occur). In addition, no source describes per-hop proofs combined with a stock-phone uniqueness primitive; the precedents (Fed SignOnce, Visa OPS, TrInc) all run custom code in a secure element or trusted application. Accurate statement: prevention against a compromised OS or app for as long as the hardware uniqueness primitive holds; a hardware break still gives unbounded double spend, and the construction itself is an inference without published precedent.
- §3 item 5, 'No phone gives apps a trusted clock' — not supportable from prior-art sources. They establish the weaker statement that offline schemes do not trust phone clocks (Bank of England 2025: security concerns about trusting smartphone clocks made time-based limits hard to implement; IMF 2025 fn 34: users can tamper with the phone's internal clock). The universal platform statement has to be sourced by the iOS and Android tracks.
- §8.3 item 6, 'No empirical compromise rate for phone key stores was found' — cannot be positively supported; it is an absence finding. Accurate statement: none appears in the BIS, IMF, ECB, Bank of England, Bundesbank, Fed or academic sources reviewed, and two web searches on 2026-10-02 returned none. This does not show that no such figure exists.
- §3 item 3 and §7.3, recovery 'can only be capped insurance' — correct under the R4 + R5 premise stated at the head of §3, but §7.3 states it without that premise. The literature has a second recovery mechanism: expiring offline value that returns automatically to the online account (Kahn, van Oordt, Zhu, Bank of Canada SWP 2021-67; BIS Polaris handbook §5.5.2 p.59, which notes it has never been used in a real setting). It requires payees to reconnect before expiry and a trustworthy clock, so R4/R5 and stock phones exclude it. Accurate statement: under R4 and R5, recovery can only be capped insurance.
- Revision 2 never states the ECB position on TEEs or names the recovery trilemma, although its reasoning depends on both. Nothing to correct, but a reader cannot check them from the proposal text. The verified facts are: ECB closing report (Oct 2025) footnote 24, TEEs are not eligible for offline payments, and footnote 27, eligible secure elements reach a level equivalent to AVA_VAN.5; Kahn, van Oordt and Zhu (2021) p.2 fn 5, at most two of offline payment functionality, no double-spending, and no loss of funds on device loss.

The 1.43 GB run had pasta_curves at O0; the 6.65 GB run was built at O3 with debug and overflow checks retained (readiness lines 873-874). Accurate statement: diagnostic builds with debug and overflow checks retained, one of them with an unoptimized curve library, on unstated hardware, with key generation included in the time. The conclusion that these are not proving-time measurements stands.
- §2.2 'App Attest and KeyMint platform classes are rejected at every boundary' is too broad. They are rejected at the three Recursive V1 boundaries (Guard verification, mint authorization, terminal authorization). The separate 'ordinary' path in the same crate requires exactly these classes (ordinary_mint_verifier.rs:95, ordinary_issuer_config.rs:46). Accurate statement: rejected at every Recursive V1 monetary boundary.
- §2.3 'a message cap in the hundreds of KB admits hash-based provers' has no end-to-end source. Published figures found: 45 KB recursive (Plonky2, laptop, 20 s), 14-145 KB non-recursive (WHIR-based, laptop), and phone proving times without proof sizes (FibRace; the Ligero credential paper). The 325-400 KB Longfellow figure in the research digest is not in its cited source. No phone measurement of a two-party recursive hash-based prover was found. Treat the sentence as an inference.
- §2.3 table row 'Receive-then-send, p95: 30 s' relabels the repo gate. The repo defines a 30,000 ms 'complete handoff' or 'overall operation' p95 (kagemusha_release_v1.rs:49-50; physical_evidence.md:63-66) and does not define it as receive-then-send. Likewise the repo's 10 s gate is 'proving p95', without 'one step'. The numbers are right; the labels are the proposal's.
- §9 'Keep it behind its existing feature' is only half right. The prover feature exists on iroha_core_zk and iroha, but connect_norito_bridge enables it unconditionally (Cargo.toml:42, 49) and has no feature of its own that removes it. A wallet build without the prover needs a bridge manifest change.
- §2.2 Pixel 6 probe: the claim is supported by the repo's written record, but the raw attestation chain is held outside the repository (readiness lines 401-402), so a reviewer cannot re-parse it. The 'no hardware counter' half is supported only as 'no app-accessible counter observed': the embedded secure element is present, no access rule was seen, and applet selection was never tested (readiness lines 351-354, 371-373).
- §2.2 'The node can verify paired-Pasta proofs' is a code-path claim only. Because no State proof has ever been produced, the node verifier has never accepted a genuine complete proof (readiness lines 297-299), and a node starts with a reject-all verifier until a release is installed (state.rs:27926-27928).
- §2.3 'Energy per payment: none defined' is right about a limit but omits that the release model already requires a recorded non-zero energy measurement per relation, helper and thermal run (kagemusha_release_v1.rs:775-776, 2691, 2820).
- §2 table 'Payment size about 7-8 KB' for B1 is an estimate, not a measurement. It matches the existing caps (7,552 B payment, 6,528 B proof) but no B1 message has been built.
- §2.3 'the Halo-style IPA family is the only one found' is a survey inference. The supporting facts check out (Mina 7,063 B proof; Pickles verifies at most two proofs on Pasta; Ragu is binary PCD with no setup and is unaudited; Nova is IVC), but the exclusivity claim itself has no source and one counter-example would refute it.
