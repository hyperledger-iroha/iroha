# Independent BOI canary ledger proposal

## Decision requested

N14 (Core, State, governance and SDKs) would own a separately versioned commissioning predecessor. N10 (Governance and Integration) would own the four-seat fixture and acceptance. This proposal starts at Iroha `5b34b8445a1864f2aa84386461c4cc0f9caec979`. The acceptance executable is the existing Linux validator with SHA-256 `4b1bbd1c406f596e1fb233bf272363bbfbbb5b87edc458dde1888afa70086c38`. BOI Core is `64553e6b8c387d697369536bd393da6c9034c59a`. The revision of this document identifies the proposed design. The executable revision must identify the implemented and qualified predecessor.

The proposed route starts at an independently signed test genesis and records every effective transition on that ledger. A genesis that directly selects the desired final state would leave an important stock read unresolved. Stock S walks the committed activation records back to its baseline, checking the universal root, network, change-set identity and effective height. Its one historical repair exception binds an existing network and authority. ## Authority and protocol scope

1. The appointing Iroha governance owner and BOI lab owner name the founding authority, four test validator custodians and the lab genesis operator that will issue scoped grants. The lab operator supplies the original signed genesis, an independently communicated expected hash, ordered BLS public keys with proofs of possession, BOI chain UUID and discriminant 369. Custodians retain signing material. An existing France or Taira holder has no implied seat on this ledger.
2. N14 specifies the founding governance rule in a distinct versioned definition. Its signed subjects bind the new genesis-derived network, universal root, predecessor, complete successor definition IDs, ordered changes, notice and effective height. The constitution names who may propose, vote and enact and how the commissioning authority ends. No host configuration switch selects a protocol. Membership, quorums, replay protection and authorization are verified from the signed founding data and committed decisions. The exact quorum for founding governance is a protocol approval item. The network’s consensus and the four-validator support requirements retain their original rules.
3. The protocol owner must resolve the bootstrap explicitly. Frozen S always founds its baseline. Its baseline has no binding ballot that can enact the next governance definition. The proposed predecessor would need a **lawful founding governance rule** and immutable definitions for its execution and result provenance. The protocol specification must identify the first authority, signed act and verifiable transition, and explain how its history reaches a release-supported baseline lineage. If it requires changing a definition already selected by S or claiming a transition that was never enacted, stop the stock-S qualification and request a different lab release pin.
4. The signed history must establish the predecessor’s complete services profile and binding set, then its required selected definitions, keyed commitment and storage, and the scoped-read permission. The known baseline has 410 bindings and services profile `df9320c3…`. The required financial predecessor has 413 and `233cb26a…`. The generic keyed and scope changes preserve those fields. Every generic change follows its genuine proposal, enactment, notice, support and effective-height rules. The deployment-bound Taira certificates remain in their original signature domain. [Native keyed construction](../crates/iroha_core/src/runtime/keyed_state.rs) · [Scoped permission](../crates/iroha_core/src/runtime/state_scope_permission.rs)
5. The predecessor must release all commissioning-only obligations before takeover. The public qualification packet must identify every extra definition, receipt and ledger record and demonstrate how unchanged S accepts the selected closure and reads its activation history. A checkpoint can authenticate a state root even when a consumer cannot interpret an earlier issuance or receipt. Whole-ledger replay and checkpoint takeover are separate test results. If a required lineage or serving operation rejects under unchanged S, the protocol owner submits a concrete revised **lab** executable for BOI review.

Check the proposed selection and every historical services transition before preparing a takeover. Extract the `selection` object from the authenticated runtime list and the ordered `transitions` array from each original change set. Retain the original responses with their hashes. Run from this checkout:

```sh
cargo run --locked --offline -p iroha_core --example boi_lab_catalog_check -- selection selection.json
cargo run --locked --offline -p iroha_core --example boi_lab_catalog_check -- transitions transitions.json
cargo test --locked --offline -p iroha_core --example boi_lab_catalog_check
```

The example calls S's compiled catalog and, for keyed selections, resolves the selected schema. S's serving policy follows activated records back to its founding baseline. An absent predecessor record yields `selected runtime lacks committed activation provenance`. An H1 activation record fails the minimum effective-height check. For a history that sets a separately named lab services profile and later restores a stock profile, the serving reader still resolves the intermediate service against the compiled catalog and rejects its unknown ID. A supported final selection alone therefore cannot qualify that history. The example exercises catalog compatibility. The stock daemon, authenticated ledger history and H+1 keyed restore supply the separate provenance and durable-state checks.
