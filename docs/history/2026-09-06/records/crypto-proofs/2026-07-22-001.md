<!-- Original context: Status / 2026-07-22 Runaway-memory containment -->
- Full-size Halo2 verifier- and proving-key generation now owns the synthesized
  circuit through breakpoint extraction, then drops that populated graph before
  key assembly. Proving-key generation reuses the supplied verifier key's
  evaluation domain instead of creating a second domain. Permutation assembly
  releases union-find scratch before polynomial construction, represents the
  omega/delta factors as one domain-row vector plus a short column vector
  instead of an n-by-m grid, and converts coefficient columns one at a time.
  Empty-bootstrap assembly retains identity implicitly and materializes
  union-find state only on the first nontrivial copy. Verifier-key construction
  now builds, commits, and drops one permutation polynomial at a time; because
  only the commitments survive in the VK, this avoids retaining the complete
  534-column permutation inventory. Verifier-key construction also finishes those streamed permutation
  commitments before assigned fixed columns and bit-packed selectors expand
  into degree-sized field polynomials, while preserving compressed and
  uncompressed processed key bytes.
  The borrowed Halo2 APIs remain available, and regression tests prove that the
  consuming path preserves the exact processed verifier- and proving-key bytes.


<a id="record-1413152c63de391cc00bbcf26b35fa94371efd476270d8db43e985a851963417"></a>

- Halo2 proof creation now consumes exactly one witness-only circuit and one
  processed proving key. It drops the circuit after witness synthesis and
  drops the domain-sized fixed-value and permutation Lagrange preprocessing
  after their final commitments. The consumed key returns its embedded VK for
  immediate proof verification and then release, eliminating the former
  circuit/full-PK borrow through transcript finalization and a duplicate
  verifier-domain parse at the peak.
