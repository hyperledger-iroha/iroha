## 2026-07-16 Contract read authentication and artifact hardening



<a id="record-dd582f84aac8faf095fe08d3e8d815815fe7c646472e7df98ede93ef73384540"></a>

- Contract identity/code and concrete multisig reads now advertise and enforce
  route-specific canonical request authentication, with Rust, JavaScript,
  Swift, Kotlin, and Java client methods signing the exact request body or GET
  target. The first-release APIs require authentication rather than retaining
  unsigned overloads.

## 2026-07-16 Executor-independent permission and role authorization



<a id="record-79726e1819ed49d004e1cabef0f5e3737840d5c5b9ac5728d9877d7a10dc5742"></a>

- Moved the deployed-contract permission boundary ahead of both Initial and
  user-provided executor dispatch on owned and borrowed overlay paths. A shared
  mutation decoder covers canonical Grant/Revoke boxes and all six concrete
  custom-registry forms. Contracts cannot register, unregister, assign, or
  mutate roles; their only account permission mutation is an exact canonical
  `CanInvokeContractEntrypoint` token bound to the immutable contract subject
  and address. The same decoder also closes concrete-form escape from the
  genesis-only `CanRegisterSmartContractCode` guard.


<a id="record-f994709a68b4985030072de2e1cdb61a6c88b7fef0955d213f214c0833c7e7fe"></a>

- Hardened role registration and membership delegation in both executors.
  Every contained permission is revalidated against the transaction authority,
  not the role destination; unknown legacy contents fail closed; governed
  genesis-only and sponsor/resource-scoped permissions cannot escape through
  role possession; and beneficiary-exact `CanUseFeeSponsorForAccount` tokens
  cannot be attached to a role. Direct fee-sponsor grants now require the live
  beneficiary/domain binding in both executors while revoke remains available
  for offboarding.
