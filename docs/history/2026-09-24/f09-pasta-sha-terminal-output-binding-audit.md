# F09 Pasta SHA-256 terminal-output binding trace — 2026-09-24

Scope: source-only review of the Paired Pasta SHA-256 queue and KAGEMUSHA recursive mint-hash claim path on `optimizations`. This is not an independent cryptographic audit or a release qualification result. No proof was generated or verified for this review.

The two similarly named job types have different roles. `PastaSha256JobV1` owns the message, eight output cells, and optional bounded geometry in the SHA queue (`crates/iroha_core/src/zk/pasta_sha256.rs:174-185`). `PastaSha256ClaimJobV1` is a borrowed ordinary-claim view (`:188-199`, constructed at `:679-694`). Production planning reads the view's message length and count, but not its `output_words` (`crates/iroha_core/src/zk/kagemusha_v1_recursion/terminal_semantic_pipeline.rs:684-715`). Its output field is read by test-only helpers (`terminal_semantic_pipeline_tests.rs:100-112`; `terminal_sha_inventory.rs:59-65`, included only under `cfg(all(test, feature = "zk-halo2-ipa"))` at `terminal_authorization.rs:5787-5789`). Thus an unused-field warning for the *claim view* is not evidence that the owned queue's SHA output is unconstrained. A future warning cleanup may gate only the view field and its initializer with `cfg(test)`, preserving the owned queue and typed-claim output fields.

For ordinary jobs, `digest_constrained` creates eight assigned output words and stores them in the owned queue (`pasta_sha256.rs:305-340`). Production synthesis iterates those owned jobs (`:775-793`), hashes the canonically padded message, and binds the final Table8 digest to `job.output_words` (`:956-964`). The binding uses physical Base-cell lookup and `region.constrain_equal` for each word (`:975-1005`). For bounded jobs, every compression-block output is copy-bound (`:918-952`), and the one-hot selected output is formed from those bound cells (`:473-501`; selector and padding constraints at `:389-409`). The Eq/Ep mint-certificate and guard circuits both invoke this SHA synthesis after Base synthesis (`kagemusha_v1_recursion/mint_helper.rs:364-380`; `guard_bundle.rs:2704-2726`).

The recursive typed-claim bridge borrows the same output cells (`pasta_sha256.rs:638-672`), range-checks them and folds them into the terminal root (`kagemusha_v1_recursion/mint_hash_claim_fold.rs:1724-1845`), then equality-binds that root to the verified claim public column (`:1866-1881`, `:1985-2015`). The typed consumer verifies the claim proof and calls that bridge (`kagemusha_v1_recursion/typed_sha_consumer.rs:187-216`, `:245-256`). The shard circuit separately binds its Table8 compression output to its public output words (`kagemusha_v1_recursion/mint_hash_shard.rs:310-328`, `:349-368`). The claim fold advances the terminal root from the shard output at final blocks (`mint_hash_claim_fold.rs:4505-4526`). The ordinary `claim_jobs()` view itself establishes none of these constraints; test-only `terminal_sha_inventory.rs:1-5` explicitly says its inventory is non-authorizing.

**Conclusion:** This trace found no production terminal digest/output-binding omission in the scoped SHA queue and recursive claim path. It does not close F09 soundness, independent review, maximum-shape, or release gates.
