# KAGEMUSHA Spartan three-mode Control soundness review

The manifest-v3 profile does not establish the proposed production security target. This addendum applies the conditional argument in [SOUNDNESS.md](SOUNDNESS.md) to the Control programs in the retained p18-s17 bundle. Claude Opus 5.5 at xhigh reviewed the mode composition and phase ordering on 19 November 2026. Claude Fable 5.2 at xhigh supplied the proposed adversary budget. Neither review supplies a quantitative reduction for concrete recursive RP57.

## What an accepting history proves

Bootstrap is the only operation with no predecessor. A Control operation has one verified wallet predecessor. Its two Boolean mode inputs at IO positions 62 and 63 select Load (0,0), Unload (1,0), or RefreshTimeAnchor (0,1). Their product must be zero. Both proofs bind the same mode through the pair link. Send and Receive continue to enforce their own mode inputs, and Receive's payer predecessor must be a Send. All three Control operations advance the wallet sequence. The four programs per curve and the selector degree in the combined matrix polynomial stay the same. The definitions are in [control.rs](../src/control.rs), the pair binding in [full.rs](../src/full.rs), and the public-mode check in [carrier.rs](../src/carrier.rs).

Fix one scheme and asset. Expand the honest history records and deduplicate repeated occurrences of the same transition under the hypotheses of SOUNDNESS §4.6. Those hypotheses cover every joined wallet, including counterparties controlled by an adversary. Custody must prevent forks and a second Bootstrap. Native must establish ledger finality for each Load. Recipient and consumed-map binding must make every successful credit correspond to a distinct Send.

Let X be the sum of Unload face amounts, L finalized Loads, D Send principal, C successful Receive credits, and F Send fees. Load adds its amount, Send subtracts principal and fee, Refresh preserves spendable value, and Unload subtracts the entire face amount. Burn adds equal amounts to the Core balance and adjusted burned total. Telescoping gives

\[
\sum_w S_w = L+C-D-F-X = L-F-X-(D-C), \qquad D-C\geq0.
\]

For the online half, let Y be total funds released from the registered reserve, including charges paid to fee beneficiaries. The additional claim \(Y\leq X\) requires World to verify each full claim for the installed scheme, asset, reserve and proof parameters, and to commit the exact net and fee legs and the replay record atomically. Exact retry returns the original result. Two payments of the same nullifier are forbidden. A proof alone cannot establish this ledger invariant. A signed Refresh establishes the floor update. Trust in elapsed time additionally requires protected direct-exchange capture and same-boot verification. [Unload](../src/unload.rs) · [RefreshTimeAnchor](../src/time_anchor.rs)

## Initial release proposal for manifest v3

Fable recommended that the chance of any invalid acceptance across one entire generation be at most \(2^{-32}\) against a combined adversary with up to \(2^{80}\) relevant oracle replies, \(2^{80}\) work units, and \(2^{48}\) distinct extracted nodes. Q includes offline work, failed attempts, and all wallets. It is a computational assumption. N includes adversarial forks as well as honest histories. Credential and sequence caps alone do not prove an N bound for the extractor. A deployment also needs a definition of the work unit, extractor cost, and allowance for every non-algebraic term.

With those Q and N, the algebraic upper bound \(Q\delta_*+N\epsilon_{\max}\) is approximately \(2^{-21.27}\). At Q = \(2^{64}\) and the same N it is approximately \(2^{-37.27}\). These are limits of the proof, not measured forgery rates. Even a per-reply bound of exactly \(2^{-112}\) would consume the entire proposed error allowance at \(2^{80}\) queries before the node and cryptographic terms.

Fable proposed a maximum five-year generation ending by 31 December 2030, followed by an Unload-only grace period. The model must count all oracle work and all accepted operations during any grace period that continues to verify this profile. Neither the calendar cutoff nor the example of \(2^{28}\) credentials times \(2^{20}\) operations is an implemented enforcement mechanism.

### Candidate full-width schedule

Fable's second review recommends two consecutive full-width RP57 squeezes. Each permutation supplies its first state word to one challenge. Under P2-RKFS B, a fresh second reply conditioned on the first has maximum atom at most 2/q on P and 1/p on S. Repeated permutation inputs require the existing consistency and collision accounting. The native transcript, recursive challenge reconstruction, signed profile and new program pins must all select the same schedule.

For s distinct formal tuple polynomials of degree at most d, fix a nonzero coefficient of the cleared numerator as a polynomial in the second challenge. Its degree in the first challenge is at most s−1. Together with the constant denominators, at most 2s−1 first-challenge values need be charged. At any remaining first challenge the nonzero numerator has degree at most d(s−1) in the second challenge. Nonconstant denominators add at most ds root events. The matrix fold is charged to the same second reply. The bounds for each group use its own maximum atom, which avoids assuming independence of two words of a single permutation.

Keeping the reviewed geometry for an estimate, the largest remaining reply is the last P inner challenge and its batching scalar. Its charge is at most 26λ₀ + 17λ₁, about 2⁻¹²⁰·⁴. The resulting algebraic lifetime estimate at Q = 2⁸⁰ and N = 2⁴⁸ is about 2⁻⁴⁰·⁴. This is a projection until the new geometry, challenge distribution and recursive encodings have been tested and independently reconciled with that ledger. The RKFS instantiation, binding and signature terms still require separate accounting.

### Charging a v4 transcript reply

For a typed squeeze input \(u\), let \(h\) contain its transcript prefix, the previously released challenge values, and the algebraic representations recorded before this squeeze. For each applicable check in SOUNDNESS §§5–7, form its *discrepancy* by subtracting the correct obligation from the claimed obligation using those representations. In a delayed sumcheck this includes the vector of unsatisfied recurrences. For a fold it includes the incoming obligations of both parents of a Receive and the new local obligation. A discrepancy that passes on to a successor is still outstanding. A nonzero discrepancy erased by evaluation is the event to charge. At the batching step, for example, a nonzero vector \((e_0,\ldots,e_d)\) is erased exactly when \(\sum_i e_i\alpha^i=0\). A false inherited matrix claim that disappears in a child's fold charges the child's challenge.

Define \(B_u(h,z)\) as the union over the check groups assigned to \(u\) of their discrepancy-erasure events on reply \(z\). For polynomial equality this requires a formally nonzero discrepancy polynomial whose value on the decoded challenge is zero. The rational lookup check includes its separately bounded pole event. The two halves read by the native transcript are projections of the same word \(z\). For a high-half check whose coefficients depend on the low half, bound the high-half root event conditional on the low half using §5's conditional atom bound. This conditioning requires that the residual is fixed independently of the high half. It cannot cover an independently chosen later commitment that already observes that half. Full-width squeezes use the reduction bound for their actual source and destination fields. [Native transcript](../src/fs.rs) · [Challenge distributions and individual checks](SOUNDNESS.md)

In the idealized experiment, stop at the first fresh input whose reply satisfies \(B_u\). If for every reachable prefix before that stop the applicable check group has conditional probability at most \(\delta_*\), the probability of a stop within \(Q\) fresh replies is at most \(Q\delta_*\), by conditioning at each step. A repeated squeeze is already fixed. Charging it only at its first occurrence requires that the erased obligations were already fixed by that occurrence's prefix and recorded representation, or that the new occurrence represents the same residual. If the same oracle input can acquire a new discrepancy after its reply is known, this stopping-time calculation does not bound that discrepancy. Justifying that identification for every recursively extracted occurrence, and providing an extraction-cost bound for the resulting representatives, remain premises of the full recursive argument. [Independent accounting review](../output/production-20261119/independent-query-accounting-opus.json)

### Last acceptance and outstanding value

The proposed five-year window describes a risk horizon. Section 6.3 of the wallet design allows a retiring wallet to remain offline indefinitely and retain receiving custody for late Payments. Ledger retirement closes future Loads for that wallet. A Payment already sent may still need a Receive and a fold before its holder can Unload. A terminal submission deadline would leave outstanding reserve liabilities with no redemption path. No such deadline has been applied. [Wallet requirements and retirement](../../../specs/kagemusha_single_design_proposal.md)

For the proposed target of \(2^{-32}\), subtracting the rounded algebraic upper bound leaves approximately \(2.32\times10^{-10}\) for all other terms together. The extractor cost polynomial and the concrete recursive RP57 advantage have no numerical bounds in the Spartan analysis. The wallet design's recursive soundness memo assumes a concrete instantiation for the PIPA-R profile. It supplies no quantitative theorem for Spartan v4. A security statement for a finite horizon must identify its end date, cover every verifier accepting within that horizon, and account separately for later redemption risk. A lifetime statement must cover the remaining liabilities until their last acceptance. [Spartan reduction](SOUNDNESS.md) · [Recursive soundness assumption](../../../specs/kagemusha_recursion_soundness_v1.md)

Chiesa and Orrù bound duplex Fiat–Shamir in an ideal-permutation model. Chen, Chiesa, Gur, O’Connor and Spooner give a proof-carrying data construction in an arithmetized random-oracle model, with an accumulation scheme for queries to that oracle. In Spartan v4 each recursive verifier evaluates fixed RP57 gates. Using either result for the outstanding term would require a reduction covering that concrete computation, the extraction cost and both parents of a joined Receive. It advances the sequence and removes the committed descriptor. The adjusted root reflects the removal only when the four evidence checks succeed. Both branches constrain the payer’s spendable balance, burned total, fee and recovery roots, and all remaining lineage fields to their prior values. ArchiveSent therefore contributes zero to each monetary term in the conservation identity above. The argument still depends on custody preserving a single payer history. The incoming receiver head adds a second parent to the extraction, including when a later receiver Send proves the original credit. ### Privacy of the published matrix claim

For the five-family generation above, a Payment publishes the folded matrix claim. Its coordinates depend on the earlier operations that contributed to the wallet state. The outgoing and return Payments in the physical iPhone round trip both passed independent native verification. Reading the originals reproduced different second-selector coordinates on both curves. The public Send statements also differ between these two Payments, so this observation alone does not quantify the additional information available to a recipient with a fixed public statement. [Matrix claim construction](../src/deferral.rs) and [recursive operation inputs](../src/full.rs)

A history-privacy argument must cover the complete published pair together with the public Payment statement and any originals that a recipient already holds. Commitment hiding and the algebraic soundness bound above each cover only part of this view. The physical round trip establishes interoperability. A history-privacy claim for this generation remains open.
